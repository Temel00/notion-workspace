# Productivity Principles

The user explicitly wants this workspace to rely on **science-based**
techniques, not just whatever feels tidy. This file is the standing
rationale — read it before proposing new structure so additions reinforce
these mechanisms instead of working against them.

## Capture: friction is the enemy
**Principle:** the harder it is to record a thought, the less reliably it
gets recorded, and unresolved open loops consume attention even when you're
not working on them (the Zeigarnik effect; see also Masicampo & Baumeister
on how merely *planning* when/where to act — not finishing the task —
relieves the intrusive-thought effect).

**Applied here:** exactly one capture point — the Inbox database inside
Habit System — with no decisions required at capture time (no Area, no
priority, nothing to fill in beyond the thought itself). Sorting into Area/
project/priority happens later, during review, not at capture. This is also
why the Dashboard/Inbox naming collision mattered enough to fix: a second
thing called "Inbox" adds friction (which one do I use?) and undermines the
one-tap guarantee.

## Planning: implementation intentions
**Principle:** "I will do X at time Y in situation Z" (an implementation
intention) predicts follow-through far better than a plain goal or to-do
item (Gollwitzer & Sheeran's meta-analysis).

**Applied here:** the Dashboard's "This Week" view and Due Date field exist
to push tasks toward a concrete when, not just a list of what. When adding
tasks during review, prefer attaching a date/time over leaving it open-ended
where the task genuinely has one.

## Habit formation: consistent cue, expect a slow ramp
**Principle:** habits form through repetition tied to a stable cue, and take
substantially longer to automate than the popular "21 days" myth suggests —
median around 66 days, with real variation by behavior and person (Lally et
al.). Missing a single occasion doesn't meaningfully harm the habit's
formation (same study).

**Applied here:** the Habit System's daily loop is built around a stable
cue/streak structure rather than perfection-tracking. Don't add friction
like guilt-inducing streak resets for a single miss — it fights the
research this system is built on.

## Review: intention–behavior gap needs a scheduled review, not willpower
**Principle:** good intentions often fail to produce action (the
intention–behavior gap); structured review points are one of the reliable
levers for closing it (Harkin et al. on self-monitoring's effect on goal
attainment).

**Applied here:** the weekly review built into Habit System is the
mechanism for reconciling what got captured/planned against what actually
happened — keep it lightweight enough that it reliably occurs (per Fogg's
work on tiny/low-friction habits being the ones that stick).

## Modes: context-based triage, not just time-of-day
**Principle:** organizing actions by *where/when you can actually do them*
rather than by project (GTD "contexts") avoids re-deciding what's doable
every time you glance at a list. Separately, switching contexts while a
task is still mentally "open" measurably drags focus into the next context
— attention residue (Leroy, 2009) — which a fixed transition ritual at the
switch point reduces.

**Applied here:** the `Mode` property (Before Work, In Car, On Lunch,
Making Dinner, At Home) is a context-based cut of Tasks, distinct from
`Area`. Each Mode has a physical/environmental cue tied to it (see Habit
System page) rather than relying on remembering to check it. The one
deliberate exception is At Home, which uses a forcing function (shoes stay
on until a task is picked) rather than a zero-friction cue — a targeted
response to a chronically-deferred category, not the default pattern to
copy elsewhere.

## Practical takeaways for future changes
- Don't multiply capture points. One inbox.
- Prefer attaching concrete dates/times to open-ended tasks when there's a
  real one, rather than leaving everything in an undated backlog.
- Don't design streaks or trackers that punish a single miss.
- Keep review-surface pages (Dashboard, weekly review) light and scannable
  — the value is in the review *happening*, not in how much the page holds.
- When a database or property is added, ask whether it reduces a decision
  at capture time or adds one — added decisions at capture time work
  against the whole design.
